## POST 1 — Founder Story (Post Tue, Week 1)

Tuesday, 11 PM. My friend closed his restaurant, sat down with a spreadsheet, and started texting customers one by one to ask for Google reviews.

He had 27 customers that day. By midnight, he'd sent about 12 texts. Most would never reply.

I asked him why he did this every single night. He said, "If I don't ask, they don't review. And if I don't have reviews, new customers don't trust me."

That moment never left me. A smart, hardworking owner spending hours every week on something that should be automatic. Not because he wanted to. Because he had no other option.

So I built ReviewPing. Automated review requests via SMS, email, and WhatsApp. Sent within 30 minutes of a customer visit. One tap to leave a review. A smart follow-up if they forget. Two-minute setup. No manual chasing.

When I showed him the first working version, he looked at me and said, "Where was this three years ago?"

If you've ever stayed late manually chasing reviews, you're exactly who I built this for.

Most owners I talk to assume automated review requests cost $399/month because that's what Podium charges. It doesn't have to.

If you're spending more than 30 minutes a week chasing reviews, ask yourself what that time is actually worth. Then look for a better way.

## POST 3 — Data/Insight (Post Wed, Week 1)

93% of customers read reviews before visiting a local business. And businesses with 50+ Google reviews see 35% more revenue.

Two numbers. One story.

The first number comes from BrightLocal 2025. It means your Google Business Profile is your new front door. Before someone walks in, they check what others are saying. If you have 8 reviews and your competitor has 80, the decision is already made.

The second number comes from Harvard Business School. It means reviews don't just build credibility. They build revenue. Directly. Every positive review is a compounding asset that works for you while you sleep.

Here's the math most owners miss: if you generate 30 new reviews per month, that's 360 in a year. The revenue impact isn't linear — it accelerates. More reviews drive more calls. More calls drive more customers. More customers drive more reviews.

Most local businesses plateau at 10-15 reviews. Not because their service is worse. Because they ask inconsistently. The difference between 15 reviews and 50 isn't quality of service. It's consistency of asking.

Check your Google profile right now. How many reviews do you have? That number is either working for you or against you.

## POST 4 — Industry-Specific (Post Thu, Week 1)

A restaurant serves 200 people a day. Maybe 2 leave a Google review. That's a 1% conversion rate.

Not because the food was bad. Because nobody asked them at the right moment.

A diner who just had a great meal is in the perfect mindset to leave a review. The food was good. The service was friendly. Thirty minutes after they walk out, that window starts closing.

This is exactly how it works for dental clinics after a procedure, salons after a haircut, auto repair shops after a pickup, and fitness studios after a class. The service is the trigger. The review request is the natural follow-through.

The best owners I know don't ask at the register anymore. They ask 30 minutes later via text or WhatsApp. The experience is still fresh. The gratitude is still there. The review link is one tap away.

If you run a service business, ask yourself: when is the last time your customers heard from you after they paid you? That gap is where reviews are either born or lost forever.
